Chapter 10 · Conservation Laws, Collisions and Gravity

These are brief answers and hints, not full solutions: they give the result and the key step, so that you can check your own work. Try each exercise properly before looking.

10.1 Newton’s Cradle

  1. After all collisions, a moving particle cannot be followed by a slower one. So if exactly one moves, it is \(A_n\), and \(\lambda mv = mu\), \(\lambda mv^2 = mu^2\) are inconsistent for \(\lambda \neq 1\). If only \(A_{n-1}\) and \(A_n\) move, the equations are those of a collision between \(m\) and \(\lambda m\), giving \(A_{n-1}\) the velocity \(\frac{1 - \lambda}{1 + \lambda}u < 0\); but then it would hit \(A_{n-2}\). Three moving particles are ruled out exactly as in part (ii). The two that move are \(A_0\), backwards at \(\frac{\lambda - 1}{\lambda + 1}u\), and \(A_n\), forwards at \(\frac{2u}{\lambda + 1}\).

  2. Equal marbles exchange velocities, so the speed \(u\) passes along to \(A_n\). \(A_n\) then hits \(A_{n+1}\): \(A_{n+1}\) leaves at \(\frac{2u}{1 + \lambda}\) and \(A_n\) rebounds at \(\frac{\lambda - 1}{\lambda + 1}u\). This backward velocity passes back along the row to \(A_0\), which leaves backwards at that speed.

  3. “Two out at \(u\)” carries momentum \(2mu\) and energy \(mu^2\), the same as before. Another solution, with no further collisions: the last three balls move off at \(\frac u3\), \(\frac u3\) and \(\frac{4u}{3}\) (check: total \(2u\), squares total \(2u^2\)). Real cradles choose the first because the balls are not quite touching, so the collisions happen one pair at a time.

  4. The speed passes along to \(A_n\), which hits the lighter \(A_{n+1}\): \(A_{n+1}\) leaves at \(\frac{2u}{1 + \lambda}\) and \(A_n\) follows more slowly at \(\frac{1 - \lambda}{1 + \lambda}u\). Nothing else moves.

10.2 The Stacked-Ball Effect

  1. The ball reaches the floor at \(U = \sqrt{2gH}\) and rebounds at \(eU\), rising \(e^2H\). With the small ball on top: the large ball, moving up at \(eU\), meets the small one moving down at \(U\). Momentum and restitution give the small ball the upward speed \(w = U\frac{Me(2 + e) - m}{M + m}\). Its centre starts at \(2R + r = 0.45\) and rises to \(4.5\), so \(\frac{w^2}{2g} = 4.05\) and \(\frac wU = \sqrt{\frac{4.05}{1.8}} = 1.5\). With \(e = \frac23\), \(\frac{16}{9}M - m = 1.5(M + m)\), giving \(M = 9m\).

  2. Momentum and restitution give \(P_1\) the speed \(\frac{1 + e}{1 + \lambda}u\); each collision repeats this with the same mass ratio, so \(P_n\) moves at \(\bigl(\frac{1 + e}{1 + \lambda}\bigr)^nu\). A particle with speed \(s\) is left with \(\frac{1 - \lambda e}{1 + \lambda}s\) after hitting the next one, which is itself slowed to \(\frac{(1 + e)(1 - \lambda e)}{(1 + \lambda)^2}s\) by its own next collision. When \(\lambda e < 1\), the first catches the second again only if \(\frac{1 - \lambda e}{1 + \lambda} > \frac{(1 + e)(1 - \lambda e)}{(1 + \lambda)^2}\), that is, \(\lambda > e\); so if \(e > \lambda\) each particle is hit once and hits once. If \(\lambda e = 1\), each particle stops dead after hitting the next.

  3. The bottom ball rebounds at \(U\) and meets the middle one (moving down at \(U\)): relative speed \(2U\), so the middle ball leaves at \(3U\). The top ball meets it with relative speed \(4U\) and leaves at \(7U\), rising \(49H\). In general \(u_{j+1} = 2u_j + U\), so \(u_j + U = 2^jU\).

  4. Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy. \(A\)’s energy is \(\frac12m\bigl(\frac{2\lambda}{1 + \lambda}\bigr)^{2n}u^2\), while the initial energy is \(\frac12\lambda^nmu^2\), and \(\frac{4\lambda}{(1 + \lambda)^2} < 1\), so \(A\) never takes more than was there. The heavy particles keep moving forwards, each with the fraction \(\frac{\lambda - 1}{\lambda + 1}\) of its speed, and keep the rest of the energy.

10.3 Rockets and Guns

  1. All at once: \(Mu = nm(v - u)\), so \(u = \frac{nmv}{M + nm}\). One at a time: the \(r\)th firing gives \(\bigl(M + (n - r + 1)m\bigr)(u_r - u_{r-1}) = mv\), so \(u_n = \sum_{j=1}^n\frac{mv}{M + jm}\), and every term except the last exceeds \(\frac{mv}{M + nm}\). So \(u_n > u\).

  2. Momentum: \(M(1 + bx)v = MV\). Then \((1 + bx)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = V\), so \(x + \frac12bx^2 = Vt\) and \(x = \frac{\sqrt{1 + 2bVt} - 1}{b}\). With the force, \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigl(M(1 + bx)v\bigr) = Mf\) gives \((1 + bx)v = ft + V\). Constant speed \(c\) needs \(x = ct\) and \((1 + bct)c = ft + V\), so \(c = V\) and \(bV^2 = f\). Otherwise \(x + \frac12bx^2 = \frac12ft^2 + Vt\), so \(x \sim t\sqrt{f/b}\) and \(v \to \sqrt{f/b}\).

  3. One stage: \(v\ln10 \approx 2.30v\). Two stages: \(v\ln\frac{10}{5.5} + v\ln\frac{5}{0.5} \approx 0.60v + 2.30v = 2.90v\).

  4. For \(0 \leq s \leq x\), \(\frac{1}{1 + x} \leq \frac{1}{1 + s} \leq 1\); integrate over \([0, x]\).

10.4 A Molecule and a Piston

  1. The recurrence telescopes to \(d_n = \frac{(v + u)d_1}{v + (2n - 1)u}\), so \(v_nd_n = \frac{(v + 2nu)(v + u)d_1}{v + (2n - 1)u}\), which exceeds \((v + u)d_1\) by \(\frac{u(v + u)d_1}{v + (2n - 1)u} \to 0\).

  2. Replace \(u\) by \(-u\): each bounce off the retreating piston costs \(2u\), so \(v_n = v - 2nu\) (while the particle can still catch the piston), \(d_n = \frac{(v - u)d_1}{v - (2n - 1)u}\), and \(v_nd_n = \frac{(v - 2nu)(v - u)d_1}{v - (2n - 1)u}\), again close to \((v - u)d_1\).

  3. With \(v = u\), \(v_n = (2n + 1)u\). At the \(n\)th collision the momentum changes by \(m(v_{n-1} + v_n) = 4nmu\), and \(t_n = \frac{d_1}{un(n + 1)}\), so the average force is \(\frac{4mu^2n^2(n + 1)}{d_1}\), of order \(n^3\). The work in each cycle is about force \(\times\ ut_n\), roughly \(4nmu^2\), and these add up to infinity: pushing the piston all the way in would need infinite work, just as the molecule’s energy \(\frac12m(2n + 1)^2u^2\) grows without limit.

  4. The invariant is \(\oint p\,\mathrm{d}q = 2\int_0^h m\sqrt{2g(h - z)}\,\mathrm{d}z \propto h^{3/2}\), where \(h\) is the height of the bounce above the floor. So as the floor rises slowly, \(h^{3/2}\) (equivalently \(v^3\), or energy times period) stays nearly constant.

10.5 Infinitely Many Bounces in Finite Time

  1. By the sine rule \(M_1N_1 = \frac{OM_1\sin30^\circ}{\sin\theta}\), and successive legs shrink by \(\rho = \frac{\sin(\theta - 30^\circ)}{\sin\theta} < 1\). The total is \(\frac{M_1N_1}{1 - \rho} = \frac{OM_1}{2\bigl(\sin\theta - \sin(\theta - 30^\circ)\bigr)} = \frac{OM_1}{4\sin15^\circ\cos(\theta - 15^\circ)}\).

  2. The angles of triangle \(OM_1N_1\) give \(\varphi = \theta - \alpha\). The time ratio is \(\frac{\rho}{k} = \frac{\sin(\theta - \alpha)\cos(\theta - \alpha)}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = \frac{\sin(2\theta - 2\alpha)}{\sin2\theta}\), which is less than \(1\) exactly when \(\alpha < \theta < 45^\circ + \frac12\alpha\).

  3. Reflecting the wedge in its walls unfolds the path into a straight line. Twelve copies of the wedge fit round \(O\), and a straight line not through \(O\) crosses at most six of them. So the particle bounces finitely often and escapes, unless it is aimed straight at \(O\).

  4. It first falls for \(t_0 = \sqrt{2/9.8} \approx 0.452\) s. Each bounce lasts \(2e^kt_0\), so the total time is \(t_0\bigl(1 + \frac{2e}{1 - e}\bigr) = 9t_0 \approx 4.07\) s. The distance is \(1 + 2\sum_{k \geq 1}e^{2k} = 1 + \frac{2(0.64)}{0.36} \approx 4.56\) m.

10.6 Two Bodies Become One

  1. The string stays taut and the relative velocity is perpendicular to it, with constant size \(u\), so \(\theta = \frac{ut}{r}\). Momentum gives \(my + MY = mut\). With \(A = G - \frac{M}{m + M}\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AB} = r(\cos\theta, -\sin\theta)\), \(y = \frac{1}{m + M}\bigl(mut + Mr\sin\frac{ut}{r}\bigr)\). Then \(\dot y = \frac{u}{m + M}\bigl(m + M\cos\frac{ut}{r}\bigr)\), which is negative when \(\cos\frac{ut}{r} < -\frac mM\), possible if \(M > m\).

  2. Now \(\ddot z = -\frac{3}{z^3}\), so \(\dot z^2 = \frac{3}{z^2} - 2\) (using \(z = 1\), \(\dot z = -1\)). With \(w = z^2\), \(\ddot w = 2\dot z^2 + 2z\ddot z = -4\), so \(z^2 = 1 - 2t - 2t^2\). They collide when \(t = \frac{\sqrt3 - 1}{2} \approx 0.366\).

  3. \(z^2 = 4t^2 - 2t + 1 = |\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}t|^2\) with \(\mathbf{p} = (1, 0)\) and \(\mathbf{q} = (-1, \sqrt3)\): the line through \((1, 0)\) in direction \((-1, \sqrt3)\), with \(\theta = \tan^{-1}\frac{\sqrt3t}{1 - t}\). Then \(z^2\dot\theta = |\mathbf{p} \times \mathbf{q}| = \sqrt3\). The repulsion is exactly the “centrifugal” term \(\frac{h^2}{z^3}\) of free motion in a plane with angular momentum \(h = \sqrt3\).

  4. The stars are at distances \(\frac{m_2a}{m_1 + m_2}\) and \(\frac{m_1a}{m_1 + m_2}\) from the centre of mass. For the first, \(\frac{Gm_1m_2}{a^2} = m_1\omega^2\frac{m_2a}{m_1 + m_2}\), so \(\omega^2 = \frac{G(m_1 + m_2)}{a^3}\) and \(T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}\).

10.7 Gravity Inside a Shell

  1. Outside (\(b > a\)), the same integral as Stage 3 runs over \(s\) from \(b - a\) to \(b + a\) and gives \(\frac{GM}{b^2}\). A solid sphere is a union of concentric shells, each acting as if its mass were at the centre.

  2. With \(r = R\cos^2\alpha\), \(\frac1r - \frac1R = \frac{\tan^2\alpha}{R}\), so the speed is \(\sqrt{2GM/R}\tan\alpha\). Then \(\mathrm{d}t = \frac{2R\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\sqrt{2GM/R}\tan\alpha} = \sqrt{\frac{R^3}{2GM}}\,2\cos^2\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\); integrate.

  3. Only the mass inside radius \(b\), which is \(M\frac{b^3}{a^3}\), pulls (by the shell theorem), giving \(\frac{GMb}{a^3}\). This is simple harmonic motion with \(\omega^2 = \frac{GM}{a^3}\), period \(2\pi\sqrt{a^3/GM} \approx 84\) minutes with \(a = 6.37 \times 10^6\) m and \(GM = 3.99 \times 10^{14}\) m\(^3\) s\(^{-2}\). A satellite at radius \(a\) has \(\omega^2a = \frac{GM}{a^2}\): the same \(\omega\).

  4. No. With \(\frac{1}{s^3}\) the integrand no longer simplifies to a constant, and the near side of the shell wins: the particle is pulled towards the nearest part of the shell. Only the inverse-square law gives exact cancellation (the area of a patch of the shell grows like \(s^2\), exactly offsetting the force).

10.8 Three Stars in a Triangle

  1. Each star is \(\frac{a}{\sqrt2}\) from the centre. The two neighbours pull with total component \(\frac{2\gamma m^2}{a^2}\cos45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt2\gamma m^2}{a^2}\) towards the centre, and the opposite star with \(\frac{\gamma m^2}{2a^2}\). Setting \(\frac{\gamma m^2}{a^2}\bigl(\sqrt2 + \frac12\bigr) = m\omega^2\frac{a}{\sqrt2}\) gives \(\omega^2 = \frac{(4 + \sqrt2)\gamma m}{2a^3}\).

  2. At the centre the pulls cancel, so the planet is in equilibrium. Along the line of the stars it is unstable: moving a distance \(x\) towards one star gives a net outward force of about \(\frac{32\gamma Mx}{a^3}\). Perpendicular to the orbital plane it is stable, with restoring force about \(\frac{16\gamma Mz}{a^3}\). (In the orbital plane, perpendicular to the line, gravity restores, but the rotation couples this motion to the unstable direction.)

  3. The force on \(m_1\) is \(\frac{\gamma m_1}{a^3}\bigl(m_2(P_2 - P_1) + m_3(P_3 - P_1)\bigr)\). Since \(m_1P_1 + m_2P_2 + m_3P_3 = (m_1 + m_2 + m_3)G\), this is \(\frac{\gamma m_1(m_1 + m_2 + m_3)}{a^3}(G - P_1)\). Every star is pulled towards \(G\) in proportion to its distance from \(G\), with the same constant, so all can rotate rigidly with \(\omega^2 = \frac{\gamma(m_1 + m_2 + m_3)}{a^3}\).

  4. The star \(k\) steps away is at distance \(2R\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}\), and its pull has component \(\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}\) towards the centre. So \(m\omega^2R = \frac{\gamma m^2}{4R^2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sin(k\pi/n)}\), giving \(\omega^2 = \frac{\gamma m}{4R^3}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sin(k\pi/n)}\). For \(n = 3\), \(R = \frac{a}{\sqrt3}\), this gives \(\frac{3\gamma m}{a^3}\).
