These are brief answers and hints, not full solutions: they give the result and the key step, so that you can check your own work. Try each exercise properly before looking.

11.1

  1. In the lift’s frame gravity is effectively \(g - f\), and the string’s stiffness is \(\frac{mg}{a}\) (it stretches by \(a\) under \(mg\)). So \(\ddot y + \frac gay = g - f\), with \(y = a\), \(\dot y = 0\) at the start. The new equilibrium is \(a - \frac{af}{g}\), so \(y = a - \frac{af}{g}(1 - \cos\omega t)\): amplitude \(\frac{af}{g}\), and the least extension \(a(1 - \frac{2f}{g})\) is positive. When the lift stops accelerating, the equilibrium returns to \(a\), and the particle is at displacement \(-\frac{af}{g}(1 - \cos\omega T)\) with velocity \(-\frac{af}{g}\omega\sin\omega T\). The new amplitude is \(\frac{af}{g}\sqrt{(1 - \cos\omega T)^2 + \sin^2\omega T} = \frac{2af}{g}\bigl|\sin\frac12\omega T\bigr|\).

  2. \(x = Ct\cos nt\) gives \(\ddot x + n^2x = -2Cn\sin nt\), so \(C = -\frac{nb}{2}\). Adding \(\frac b2\sin nt\) to meet the initial conditions: \(x = \frac b2(\sin nt - nt\cos nt)\). L’Hôpital’s rule in \(p\), applied to the formula in the question, gives the same as \(p \to n\).

  3. \(n\sin pt - p\sin nt = n(\sin pt - \sin nt) + (n - p)\sin nt = 2n\cos\frac{(n + p)t}{2}\sin\frac{(p - n)t}{2} + (n - p)\sin nt\). The second term is small. The first is a fast oscillation inside the slowly varying envelope \(\bigl|\sin\frac{(n - p)t}{2}\bigr|\), which repeats every \(\frac{2\pi}{n - p}\).

  4. The amplitude is \(\frac{F}{\sqrt{(n^2 - p^2)^2 + 4k^2p^2}}\). Minimising the denominator over \(p^2\): \(-2(n^2 - p^2) + 4k^2 = 0\), so \(p^2 = n^2 - 2k^2\).

11.2Modes

  1. Differentiating, the energy changes at rate \(\dot x\bigl(m\ddot x + \frac\lambda a(2x + y)\bigr) + \dot y\bigl(m\ddot y + \frac\lambda a(x + 2y)\bigr) = 0\). With \(u = x - y\) and \(v = x + y\), the energy is \(\bigl(\frac m4\dot u^2 + \frac{\lambda}{4a}u^2\bigr) + \bigl(\frac m4\dot v^2 + \frac{3\lambda}{4a}v^2\bigr)\), and each bracket is the energy of a simple harmonic oscillator, so each is constant.

  2. Period \(\frac{2\pi}{\omega}\): start with \(x = -y\) and \(\dot x = -\dot y\) (so that \(v \equiv 0\)). Period \(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3\omega}\): start with \(x = y\) and \(\dot x = \dot y\).

  3. The force on \(X\) is \(-\frac\lambda ax - \frac\mu a(x + y)\). The mode \(x - y\) still has \(\omega^2 = \frac{\lambda}{ma}\), and \(x + y\) now has \(\frac{\lambda + 2\mu}{ma}\). Every motion is periodic exactly when the ratio of frequencies \(\sqrt{1 + 2\mu/\lambda}\) is rational.

  4. The matrix is \(\frac km\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvectors \((1, \sqrt2, 1)\), \((1, 0, -1)\) and \((1, -\sqrt2, 1)\) for \(2 - \sqrt2\), \(2\) and \(2 + \sqrt2\). The modes: all three swing together, the middle one most; the outer two swing oppositely with the middle at rest; and the middle swings against the outer two.

11.3Real Pendulum

  1. Energy: \(\frac12m\dot x^2 + \frac{\lambda}{2a}\bigl(\sqrt{x^2 + a^2} - a\bigr)^2 = \frac12mv^2\). The greatest displacement has \(\sqrt{x^2 + a^2} = a + \frac vk\), so \(x_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{2av}{k} + \frac{v^2}{k^2}}\). For small \(v\) the extension is about \(\frac{x^2}{2a}\), so \(\dot x^2 \approx v^2 - \frac{k^2x^4}{4a^2}\). With \(x = Xu\) and \(X = \sqrt{2av/k}\), the period \(4\int_0^X\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\dot x}\) becomes \(\frac{4X}{v}\int_0^1\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\sqrt{1 - u^4}}\). The restoring force is proportional to \(x^3\), not \(x\) (at \(x = 0\) the string is exactly at its natural length), so there is no simple harmonic limit.

  2. \((1 - m\sin^2\phi)^{-1/2} \approx 1 + \frac m2\sin^2\phi + \frac{3m^2}{8}\sin^4\phi\) with \(m = \sin^2\frac\gamma2 \approx \frac{\gamma^2}{4} - \frac{\gamma^4}{48}\). Averaging over \(\phi\) gives \(1 + \frac m4 + \frac{9m^2}{64}\), and the \(\gamma^4\) terms are \(-\frac{1}{192} + \frac{9}{1024} = \frac{11}{3072}\).

  3. Starting from \(1\) and \(\cos45^\circ = 0.70711\): \((0.85355, 0.84090)\), then \((0.84722, 0.84720)\), then \(0.847213\). The period factor is \(\frac{1}{0.847213} = 1.18034\).

  4. The period factor changes by \(\frac{1}{16}(\gamma_5^2 - \gamma_4^2) \approx 1.71 \times 10^{-4}\) (in radians), so the clock loses about \(15\) seconds a day.

11.4Tautochrone

  1. Since \(y = \frac{s^2}{4k}\), \(\ddot s = -g\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}s} = -\frac{g}{2k}s\): simple harmonic motion with \(\omega^2 = \frac{g}{2k}\). From \(s_0 = 2\sqrt{kh}\), the speed at \(V\) is \(\omega s_0 = \sqrt{2gh}\).

  2. The bowl is a pendulum of length \(R\). From a small height the time to the bottom is \(\frac\pi2\sqrt{R/g}\). From the rim (\(\gamma = 90^\circ\)) it is \(1.180\) times longer, about \(1.85\sqrt{R/g}\).

  3. Yes. Both reach \(V\) after a quarter period and collide there. Whatever velocities they leave with, each performs simple harmonic motion in \(s\) with the same period, so each returns to \(V\) after exactly half a period, and they collide at \(V\) again. (A particle left at rest at \(V\) simply stays there.)

  4. With \(x = R(\theta - \sin\theta)\) and \(y = R(1 - \cos\theta)\), \(\mathrm{d}s = 2R\sin\frac\theta2\,\mathrm{d}\theta\), so one arch has length \(\int_0^{2\pi}2R\sin\frac\theta2\,\mathrm{d}\theta = 8R\). The area is \(\int y\,\mathrm{d}x = R^2\int_0^{2\pi}(1 - \cos\theta)^2\,\mathrm{d}\theta = 3\pi R^2\).

11.5in an Accelerating Train

  1. In the carriage’s frame there is a backward (southward) force \(a\) per unit mass, in the direction of firing. With flight time \(\frac{2V\sin\theta}{g}\), the range is \(\frac{V^2}{g}\bigl(\sin2\theta + \frac ag(1 - \cos2\theta)\bigr)\). Its derivative vanishes when \(\tan2\theta = -\frac ga\), so \(2\theta \approx \frac\pi2 + \frac ag\). The maximum is \(\frac{V^2}{g}\bigl(\sqrt{1 + a^2/g^2} + \frac ag\bigr) \approx \frac{V^2}{g} + \frac{V^2a}{g^2}\).

  2. \(x = ut\cos\theta - \frac12ft^2\), landing at \(t = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\) and \(x = \frac{2u^2\sin\theta}{g^2}(g\cos\theta - f\sin\theta)\). The horizontal velocity \(u\cos\theta - ft\) turns negative before landing if \(\tan\theta > \frac{g}{2f}\), so \(\alpha = \tan^{-1}\frac{g}{2f}\). With \(f = g\) and \(\theta = 45^\circ\), the acceleration is exactly opposite to the initial velocity: the projectile goes straight up the line \(y = x\), stops, and comes straight back to \(O\).

  3. The effective gravity is \(g\) downwards plus \(a\) backwards. The free surface is perpendicular to it, so it is tilted at \(\tan^{-1}\frac ag\).

  4. The effective gravity has size \(\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}\), and at \(x = \frac{ak}{2g}\) the slope is \(\frac ag\) and the radius of curvature is \(\rho = \frac{k}{2}\bigl(1 + \frac{a^2}{g^2}\bigr)^{3/2}\). The period is \(2\pi\sqrt{\rho/\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{k(g^2 + a^2)}{2g^3}}\), which reduces to \(2\pi\sqrt{\frac{k}{2g}}\) when \(a = 0\).

11.6

  1. Friction acts at the contact point, so angular momentum about that point is conserved: \(maV + \frac25ma^2\frac Va = mav_0 + \frac25ma^2\omega_0\). So \(V = \frac{5v_0 + 2a\omega_0}{7}\), which exceeds \(v_0\) when \(\frac{v_0}{a\omega_0} < 1\).

  2. \(V = \frac57v_0\). The final energy is \(\frac12m\bigl(\frac57v_0\bigr)^2\bigl(1 + \frac25\bigr) = \frac57 \cdot \frac12mv_0^2\), so \(\frac27\) of the energy is lost.

  3. The same argument with \(I = ma^2\) gives \(V = \frac12(v_0 - a\omega_0)\). Setting \(V = -\frac{v_0}{7}\) gives \(\frac{v_0}{a\omega_0} = \frac79\).

  4. An impulse \(J\) at height \(h\) above the centre gives \(mv = J\) and \(\frac25ma^2\omega = Jh\). Rolling needs \(v = a\omega\), so \(h = \frac25a\).

11.7’s Rule in a Rod

  1. \(\frac16\bigl(1 + 4 \cdot \frac45 + \frac12\bigr) = \frac{47}{60} \approx 0.7833\), against \(\frac\pi4 \approx 0.7854\). For \(\int_{-1}^1\), the rule \(\frac13\bigl(\mathrm{f}(-1) + 4\mathrm{f}(0) + \mathrm{f}(1)\bigr)\) gives \(0\) for \(x^3\) and \(\frac23\) for \(x^2\) (both exact), but \(\frac23\) for \(x^4\), whose integral is \(\frac25\).

  2. The moment of inertia about the axis at angle \(\psi\) is a quadratic form \(A\cos^2\psi + B\sin^2\psi + 2C\sin\psi\cos\psi\). Invariance under \(\psi \mapsto \psi + 90^\circ\) forces \(A = B\) and \(C = 0\), so it is the same for every axis: \(\frac13Ma^2\), the value about the axis parallel to a side.

  3. Placing \(\frac13\) of the area at each midpoint integrates every quadratic exactly over a triangle (check \(1\), \(x\), \(x^2\), \(xy\) on a right triangle). So the three particles have the same mass, centre of mass and second moments as the lamina.

  4. Take products of the rod weights \(\frac16\), \(\frac23\), \(\frac16\) at \(-a\), \(0\), \(a\) in each direction: \(\frac{M}{216}\) at the corners, \(\frac{M}{54}\) at the edge midpoints, \(\frac{2M}{27}\) at the face centres and \(\frac{8M}{27}\) at the centre. About an axis through the centre parallel to an edge, \(I = M\bigl(\frac{a^2}{3} + \frac{a^2}{3}\bigr) = \frac23Ma^2\). By the parallel axis theorem, about an edge it is \(\frac23Ma^2 + M(\sqrt2a)^2 = \frac83Ma^2\).

11.8Equilibria

  1. Measured from the common face towards the cone, \(G\) is at \(\frac{-\frac23 \cdot \frac{3a}{8} + \frac\lambda3 \cdot \frac{\lambda a}{4}}{\frac23 + \frac\lambda3} = \frac{a(\lambda^2 - 3)}{4(2 + \lambda)}\). On the hemisphere the normal at the contact point always passes through the centre of the face, so the toy rights itself when \(G\) is below that centre, that is, \(\lambda < \sqrt3\). On its side, the toy touches the table along a generator of the cone and at the rim, and it stays there if the vertical through \(G\) falls between the apex and the rim. This needs the height of \(G\) to be at least \(-\frac a\lambda\), which reduces to \(\lambda^3 + \lambda + 8 > 0\): always true.

  2. Every point of a uniform sphere is an equilibrium (neutral). If the centre of mass is slightly off-centre, there are just two: with \(G\) lowest (stable) and with \(G\) highest (unstable).

  3. Equilibria occur where the normal passes through the centre: at the four ends of the axes. Resting on an end of the minor axis is stable (\(G\) as low as possible); on an end of the major axis, unstable.

  4. By symmetry \(G\) is at the origin. For \(n = 2\), \(Y = r\bigl(\frac12 - p^2\bigr)\), and a tilted equilibrium needs the normal to pass through \(G\), so \(Y = 0\) and \(p = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\). There the slope is \(-\sqrt2\), so \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) is tilted at \(\tan^{-1}\sqrt2 \approx 54.7^\circ\), or \(125.3^\circ\) when resting on the other paraboloid. The two are reflections of each other in the \(x\)-axis, which is why the tilts pair up.

11.9Safety Parabola

  1. Each particle is at \(ut\,\hat{\mathbf{n}} - \frac12gt^2\mathbf{j}\), a sphere of radius \(ut\) centred at \(-\frac12gt^2\mathbf{j}\). Fired horizontally from \((0, \frac{u^2}{2g})\), a particle follows \(y = \frac{u^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}\): exactly the parabola of safety. The particle fired at angle \(\alpha\) touches it at \(x = \frac{u^2}{g\tan\alpha}\) at time \(\frac{u}{g\sin\alpha}\); the horizontal particle takes \(\frac{x}{u}\) to get there, so it must be launched at time \(\frac{u(1 - \cos\alpha)}{g\sin\alpha} = \frac ug\tan\frac\alpha2\) (this needs \(\alpha \geq 45^\circ\), so that the touching point is above the ground).

  2. The line meets the parabola of safety where \(R\sin\beta = \frac{u^2}{2g} - \frac{gR^2\cos^2\beta}{2u^2}\), giving \(R = \frac{u^2}{g(1 + \sin\beta)}\). At the maximum the two angles of Exercise 3 coincide, so the best angle is the bisector, \(45^\circ + \frac12\beta\).

  3. With \(\lambda = \tan\theta\), the two values satisfy \(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 = \frac{2u^2}{gx}\) and \(\lambda_1\lambda_2 = 1 + \frac{2u^2y}{gx^2}\). So \(\tan(\theta_1 + \theta_2) = -\frac xy\) and \(\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ + \varphi\), where \(\varphi\) is the elevation of \(OP\). The bisector of \(OP\) and the vertical is at \(\frac12(90^\circ + \varphi)\).

  4. The vertex is at \(\bigl(\frac{u^2}{2g}\sin2\theta, \frac{u^2}{2g}\sin^2\theta\bigr)\) and the focal length is \(\frac{u^2\cos^2\theta}{2g}\), so the focus is at height \(\frac{u^2}{2g}(\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta)\). Its distance from \(O\) is \(\frac{u^2}{2g}\), whatever \(\theta\).

11.10Ball That Comes Back Towards You

  1. In units \(u = g = 1\), \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}OP^2 = t(t^2 - 3t\sin\alpha + 2)\), with roots \(t \approx 1.050\) (local maximum, \(OP \approx 0.516\frac{u^2}{g}\)) and \(t \approx 1.904\) (local minimum, \(OP \approx 0.336\frac{u^2}{g}\)). The ball lands at \(t \approx 1.970\).

  2. Resolving along and perpendicular to the plane, \(OP^2 = u^2t^2 - ugt^3\sin(\alpha' + \beta) + \frac14g^2t^4\), where \(\alpha'\) is measured from the plane. This is the level-ground formula with \(\alpha = \alpha' + \beta\). So the condition \(\sin(\alpha' + \beta) > \frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\) concerns the angle from the horizontal; measured from the plane, the critical angle drops to \(70.5^\circ - \beta\) (provided the ball has not already landed).

  3. Smaller. Drag kills the horizontal motion and makes the descent steeper, so the ball drops back more nearly vertically, which makes it easier for \(OP\) to shrink.

  4. The formula for \(OP^2\) does not involve the landing point, so \(OP\) decreases for a time exactly when \(\sin\alpha > \frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\), as before (and the longer flight means the decreasing phase always happens before landing). Eventually the fall dominates and \(OP\) increases.

11.11Fielders Catch

  1. If she stands at \(D_0\) and runs at velocity \(v\) away from the batsman, the ball is at distance \(D_0 + vt - Ut\) in front of her. To catch it she needs \(D_0 + vT = UT\), and then the distance is \((U - v)(T - t)\), so \(\tan\theta = \frac{gt}{2(U - v)}\), again linear. If the ball lands behind her, \(UT > D_0\) and \(v > 0\): she runs backwards.

  2. \(\theta = \tan^{-1}\frac{gt}{2U}\), so \(\dot\theta = \frac{g/2U}{1 + (gt/2U)^2}\), which decreases. \(\tan\theta\) is linear because the height \(\frac12gt(T - t)\) and the distance \(U(T - t)\) share the factor \(T - t\).

  3. Yes. The height relative to her eye is a quadratic that vanishes at the catch, so it factorises as \((T - t)(c + dt)\); dividing by \(U(T - t)\) leaves a linear function (which starts negative: she first looks down).

  4. No. With drag, the horizontal distance is \(\frac Uk(\mathrm{e}^{-kt} - \mathrm{e}^{-kT})\), and the height is not this times a linear function of \(t\), so \(\tan\theta\) is no longer linear.

11.12Ant on the Rubber Rope

  1. \(\frac{x}{a + ut} = \frac1k\ln\frac{a + ut}{a}\), which is \(\frac12\) at \(t = \frac au(\mathrm{e}^{k/2} - 1)\). Since \(\frac T2 = \frac{a}{2u}(\mathrm{e}^{k/2} - 1)(\mathrm{e}^{k/2} + 1)\) and \(\frac12(\mathrm{e}^{k/2} + 1) > 1\), this is before \(\frac T2\).

  2. \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac xL = \frac vL = \frac va\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\), so \(\frac xL = \frac{v}{a\lambda}(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t})\), which reaches \(1\) only if \(v > \lambda a\).

  3. \(\frac xL = v\int_0^t\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{a + \frac12bs^2}\), which tends to \(\frac{v\pi}{\sqrt{2ab}}\). So the ant arrives only if \(v > \frac{\sqrt{2ab}}{\pi}\).

  4. It needs \(\frac{H_N}{100\,000} = \frac{1}{100}\), so \(H_N = 1000\) and \(N \approx \mathrm{e}^{999.4} \approx 10^{434}\) seconds.

11.13Snowplough Problem

  1. The depth at time \(t\) after noon is proportional to \(t + t_0\), so the distance covered is \(c\ln\frac{t + t_0}{t_0}\). The conditions \(x(1) = 2\) and \(x(2) = 3\) give \(\bigl(1 + \frac{1}{t_0}\bigr)^3 = \bigl(1 + \frac{2}{t_0}\bigr)^2\). With \(u = \frac{1}{t_0}\) this reduces to \(u^2 - u - 1 = 0\), so \(u = \frac{1 + \sqrt5}{2}\) and \(t_0 \approx 0.618\) hours: it started snowing at about 11:23.

  2. \(\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}y} = \frac{\alpha h}{k}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha y} - \frac{\alpha h}{k}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha y} + \alpha\bigl(T - \frac{\alpha hy}{k}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha y}\), and \(\alpha\bigl(t - (\mathrm{e}^{\alpha y} - 1)\frac hk\bigr)\) is the same.

  3. The gap is \(\bigl(T - \frac{\alpha hy}{k}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha y}\), whose derivative vanishes at \(y = \frac{kT - h}{\alpha h}\). If \(kT > h\), the gap grows until then and shrinks to \(0\) at \(y = \frac{kT}{\alpha h}\). The second plough faces snow of depth \(k \times\) (gap); while this is deeper than the snow the first plough met at the same place, it falls further behind.

  4. \(\frac1\alpha\frac{\mathrm{d}t_3}{\mathrm{d}y} = t_3 - t_2(y)\). Writing \(t_3 = t_2 + g\) gives \(g = \mathrm{e}^{\alpha y}\bigl(T - \alpha Ty + \frac{\alpha^2hy^2}{2k}\bigr)\). If \(kT \geq 2h\), this vanishes at \(y = \frac{k}{\alpha h}\bigl(T - \sqrt{T^2 - 2hT/k}\bigr)\), before the second plough catches the first; if \(kT < 2h\), the second catches the first first.

11.14, Not Catenary

  1. Yes. The horizontal tension \(H\) is still constant, the middle segment has slope \(0\), and the slopes then go up in steps of \(\frac wH\). With \(x\) measured from the middle of the horizontal segment, the \(k\)th joint is at \(x = (k - \frac12)h\) and height \(\frac{wh}{2H}k(k - 1)\) above the lowest joints, which is \(\frac{w}{2Hh}\bigl(x^2 - \frac{h^2}{4}\bigr)\): a parabola.

  2. The tension is \(\sqrt{H^2 + V^2}\), greatest where the vertical component \(V\) is greatest: in the end segments, where \(V\) supports half the load, \(\frac{(2n + 1)w}{2}\). So \(T_{\max} = \sqrt{H^2 + \frac14(2n + 1)^2w^2}\).

  3. \(y' = \sinh\frac xc\) and \(y'' = \frac1c\cosh\frac xc = \frac1c\sqrt{1 + \sinh^2\frac xc}\). The length is \(\int_0^X\cosh\frac xc\,\mathrm{d}x = c\sinh\frac Xc\).

  4. For a shallow curve with sag \(s\), both the catenary and the parabola have length about \(2d + \frac{4s^2}{3d}\), so both give \(s \approx \sqrt{\frac32d(L - d)}\); they differ only at order \(s^4\). For a nearly taut chain, the difference is far smaller than anything Galileo could have measured.
