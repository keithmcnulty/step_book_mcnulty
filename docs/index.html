Beyond STEP

Past STEP questions as a doorway to university mathematics

STEP is the hardest mathematics examination most school students ever sit. It is also one of the best: its questions are short, unfamiliar and deep, and the best of them are small pieces of university mathematics in disguise.

This book takes the most interesting STEP questions, from the first papers in 1987 (and the specimen papers that preceded them) to the present, and uses each one as a doorway. We solve the question carefully, asking the questions a good student asks along the way, and then step through the door: where the idea comes from, what it turns into at university, and what to read next.

It is written for talented aspiring mathematicians preparing for STEP who want more than practice papers: to start thinking like a mathematics undergraduate, and to find out what is waiting for them.

The book treats 136 questions in full. For every STEP question, more than 1,500 of them from the specimen papers to 2025, there is a worked solution in a similar style but much shorter: the key idea, a complete solution part by part, checks you can make, common slips, and a pointer to the university mathematics behind the question.

How each section works

  1. The questionA link to the original STEP question at its official source. Open it and try it first, for at least half an hour.
  2. The hookWhy this question is more interesting than it looks.
  3. Working it throughA guided solution in stages, with questions to ask yourself along the way.
  4. What’s really going onThe university mathematics hiding behind the question.
  5. The thinking skillThe habit of mind the question trains, which you will use again and again.
  6. Try this nextFollow-up problems, and reading suitable for a first-year undergraduate.
