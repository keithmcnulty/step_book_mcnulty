Mathematical Symbols

Download as PDFtypeset for print

Mathematical writing packs a lot of meaning into a few marks, and university lecturers use many symbols that school textbooks rarely do. The tables below list the symbols used in this book, grouped by topic, with what each one means and how it is read aloud. Most of them will become second nature within a few weeks of starting a degree; until then, this is somewhere to look them up.

Two conventions are worth knowing from the start. First, this book follows STEP in printing some letters upright rather than in italics when they have a fixed meaning: \(\mathrm{e}\) for the base of natural logarithms, \(\mathrm{i}\) for \(\sqrt{-1}\), \(\mathrm{d}\) in \(\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}x\), and the names of functions, as in \(\mathrm{f}(x)\) and \(\mathrm{P}(A)\). Italic letters are variables. Secondly, the same symbol sometimes means different things in different contexts; \(\equiv\) and \(\sim\) are the main culprits, and the tables point out where.

Logic and proof

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(P \Rightarrow Q\) \(P\) implies \(Q\): if \(P\) is true, so is \(Q\) \(x = 2 \Rightarrow x^2 = 4\); “\(P\) implies \(Q\)”
\(P \Leftarrow Q\) \(P\) is implied by \(Q\) “\(P\) if \(Q\)”
\(P \iff Q\) \(P\) if and only if \(Q\): each implies the other \(x^2 = 4 \iff x = \pm2\); also written “iff”
\(\forall\) for all, for every \(\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\ x^2 \geq 0\)
\(\exists\) there exists \(\exists\, n \in \mathbb{Z}\) with \(n^2 = 9\)
\(\neg P\) not \(P\), the negation of \(P\) the negation of “\(\forall x,\ P(x)\)” is “\(\exists\, x\) with \(\neg P(x)\)”
\(:=\) is defined to equal \(\mathrm{f}(x) := x^2 + 1\)
\(\blacksquare\) end of proof; also written QED from the Latin quod erat demonstrandum, “which was to be shown”

Sets and number systems

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(\{\,\ldots\}\) the set whose elements are listed \(\{1, 2, 3\}\)
\(\{x : \ldots\}\) the set of all \(x\) such that …; also written \(\{x \mid \ldots\}\) \(\{x : x^2 < 2\}\); “the set of \(x\) such that \(x^2 < 2\)”
\(x \in A\) \(x\) is an element of \(A\) \(\sqrt2 \in \mathbb{R}\); “\(\sqrt2\) is in \(\mathbb{R}\)”
\(x \notin A\) \(x\) is not an element of \(A\) \(\sqrt2 \notin \mathbb{Q}\)
\(A \subseteq B\) \(A\) is a subset of \(B\): every element of \(A\) is in \(B\) \(\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}\)
\(A \cup B\) union: elements in \(A\) or \(B\) (or both) \(\{1, 2\} \cup \{2, 3\} = \{1, 2, 3\}\)
\(A \cap B\) intersection: elements in both \(A\) and \(B\) \(\{1, 2\} \cap \{2, 3\} = \{2\}\)
\(A \setminus B\) elements of \(A\) that are not in \(B\) \(\{1, 2, 3\} \setminus \{2\} = \{1, 3\}\)
\(A'\) complement: everything not in \(A\) also written \(A^\mathrm{c}\) or \(\bar A\)
\(\varnothing\) the empty set, with no elements \(\{x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 < 0\} = \varnothing\)
\(|A|\) the number of elements of a finite set \(A\) \(|\{a, b, c\}| = 3\)
\(A \times B\) the set of all pairs \((a, b)\) with \(a \in A\), \(b \in B\) \(\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}\) is the plane, also written \(\mathbb{R}^2\)
\(\mathbb{N}\) the natural numbers \(1, 2, 3, \ldots\) some authors include \(0\)
\(\mathbb{Z}\) the integers \(\ldots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \ldots\) from the German Zahlen, “numbers”
\(\mathbb{Q}\) the rational numbers (fractions) from quotient
\(\mathbb{R}\) the real numbers
\(\mathbb{C}\) the complex numbers
\(\mathbb{H}\) the quaternions after Hamilton (Section 6.7)
\(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) numbers \(a + b\mathrm{i}\) with \(a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\) the Gaussian integers (Section 1.6)
\([a, b]\) closed interval: \(a \leq x \leq b\) includes its endpoints
\((a, b)\) open interval: \(a < x < b\) excludes its endpoints; also a pair or a point

Numbers, divisibility and approximation

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(a \mid b\) \(a\) divides \(b\): \(b\) is a whole-number multiple of \(a\) \(3 \mid 12\); “\(3\) divides \(12\)”
\(a \nmid b\) \(a\) does not divide \(b\) \(5 \nmid 12\)
\(a \equiv b \pmod n\) \(a\) and \(b\) leave the same remainder on division by \(n\) \(17 \equiv 2 \pmod 5\); “\(17\) is congruent to \(2\) modulo \(5\)”
\(\equiv\) (without a modulus) is identically equal to: true for all values \((x + 1)^2 \equiv x^2 + 2x + 1\)
\(\lfloor x \rfloor\) floor: the greatest integer \(\leq x\) \(\lfloor 3.7 \rfloor = 3\), \(\lfloor -3.7 \rfloor = -4\)
\(\lceil x \rceil\) ceiling: the least integer \(\geq x\) \(\lceil 3.2 \rceil = 4\)
\(n!\) \(n\) factorial, \(1 \times 2 \times \cdots \times n\) \(0! = 1\) by convention
\(\binom{n}{r}\) the binomial coefficient \(\frac{n!}{r!\,(n - r)!}\) “\(n\) choose \(r\)”; also written \({}^n\mathrm{C}_r\)
\(\approx\) is approximately equal to \(\pi \approx 3.1416\)
\(\sim\) is asymptotic to: the ratio tends to \(1\) \(n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/\mathrm{e})^n\) (but see also Probability)
\(\propto\) is proportional to \(v \propto 1/d\)
\(\pm\), \(\mp\) plus or minus; minus or plus \(\cos(A \pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B\)
\(0.\overline{01}\) recurring digits \(0.\overline{01} = 0.010101\ldots\)

Functions

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(\mathrm{f} : A \to B\) \(\mathrm{f}\) is a function from the set \(A\) to the set \(B\) \(\mathrm{f} : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\)
\(x \mapsto \ldots\) \(x\) is sent to … \(x \mapsto x^2 - 2\); “\(x\) maps to \(x^2 - 2\)”
\(\mathrm{f} \circ \mathrm{g}\) composition: do \(\mathrm{g}\), then \(\mathrm{f}\) \((\mathrm{f} \circ \mathrm{g})(x) = \mathrm{f}(\mathrm{g}(x))\)
\(\mathrm{f}^{-1}\) the inverse function of \(\mathrm{f}\) not \(1/\mathrm{f}\)
\(\mathrm{f}^n\) in this book, usually \(\mathrm{f}\) applied \(n\) times \(\mathrm{f}^3(x) = \mathrm{f}(\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{f}(x)))\); but \(\sin^2 x\) means \((\sin x)^2\)
\(|x|\) modulus, or absolute value \(|-3| = 3\)
\(\exp x\) the exponential function \(\mathrm{e}^x\)
\(\ln x\) natural logarithm, to base \(\mathrm{e}\) at university often written \(\log x\)
\(\sec\), \(\mathrm{cosec}\), \(\cot\) \(1/\cos\), \(1/\sin\), \(1/\tan\)
\(\arcsin\), \(\arccos\), \(\arctan\) inverse trigonometric functions also written \(\sin^{-1}\), etc.
\(\sinh\), \(\cosh\), \(\tanh\) hyperbolic functions: \(\sinh x = \frac12(\mathrm{e}^x - \mathrm{e}^{-x})\), \(\cosh x = \frac12(\mathrm{e}^x + \mathrm{e}^{-x})\), \(\tanh = \sinh/\cosh\) read “shine” (or “sinch”), “cosh” and “than” (or “tanch”)
\(\operatorname{artanh}\) inverse of \(\tanh\) \(\operatorname{artanh} x = \frac12\ln\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}\)
\(\max\), \(\min\) the largest, smallest of \(\max(3, 7) = 7\)
\(\Gamma(x)\) the gamma function \(\Gamma(n + 1) = n!\) (Section 4.4)

Sums, limits and calculus

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(\sum\) sum \(\sum_{k=1}^n k = \frac12n(n + 1)\)
\(\prod\) product \(\prod_{k=1}^n k = n!\)
\(\infty\) infinity: not a number, but shorthand for “without limit” \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac1{k^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}\)
\(x \to a\) \(x\) tends to \(a\) \(\frac1n \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\)
\(\lim\) limit \(\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x} = 1\)
\(\mathrm{f}'(x)\), \(\mathrm{f}''(x)\) first and second derivatives “f dash”, or “f prime”
\(\dot x\), \(\ddot x\) derivatives with respect to time \(\dot x = \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\); “\(x\) dot”, “\(x\) double dot”
\(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\) partial derivative: differentiate with respect to \(x\), holding other variables fixed “partial \(f\) by partial \(x\)”
\(\int_a^b\) definite integral from \(a\) to \(b\)
\(\oint\) integral round a closed loop \(\oint p\,\mathrm{d}q\) (Section 10.4)
\(\delta x\), \(\Delta x\) a small change in \(x\); a change in \(x\)
\(\varepsilon\), \(\delta\) conventional names for small positive numbers in analysis “for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a \(\delta > 0\) such that …”

Complex numbers

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(\mathrm{i}\) a square root of \(-1\) engineers write \(\mathrm{j}\)
\(\operatorname{Re} z\), \(\operatorname{Im} z\) real and imaginary parts if \(z = 3 + 4\mathrm{i}\), \(\operatorname{Re} z = 3\), \(\operatorname{Im} z = 4\)
\(z^*\), \(\bar z\) complex conjugate \((3 + 4\mathrm{i})^* = 3 - 4\mathrm{i}\); both notations are used
\(|z|\) modulus: distance from \(0\) \(|3 + 4\mathrm{i}| = 5\)
\(\arg z\) argument: angle from the positive real axis \(\arg \mathrm{i} = \frac\pi2\)
\(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\) \(\cos\theta + \mathrm{i}\sin\theta\) \(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi} = -1\)
\(\omega\) conventionally, a complex cube root of unity \(\omega = \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\), \(1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0\)

Vectors and matrices

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(\mathbf{a}\), \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) a vector; the vector from \(A\) to \(B\) handwritten as \(\underline{a}\)
\(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) a unit vector, of length \(1\) “\(n\) hat”
\(|\mathbf{a}|\) the length (magnitude) of \(\mathbf{a}\)
\(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}\) scalar (dot) product \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta\)
\(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\) vector (cross) product perpendicular to \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\)
\(\mathbf{M}\) a matrix
\(\mathbf{I}\) the identity matrix \(\mathbf{I}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}\) for every \(\mathbf{v}\)
\(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) the inverse matrix \(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{-1} = \mathbf{I}\)
\(\det \mathbf{M}\) the determinant of \(\mathbf{M}\) \(\det\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix} = ad - bc\)
\(\operatorname{tr} \mathbf{M}\) the trace: sum of the diagonal entries \(\operatorname{tr}\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix} = a + d\)
\(\lambda\) conventionally, an eigenvalue \(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}\)

Probability and statistics

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(\mathrm{P}(A)\) the probability of the event \(A\)
\(\mathrm{P}(A \mid B)\) the probability of \(A\) given \(B\) \(\mathrm{P}(A \mid B) = \mathrm{P}(A \cap B)/\mathrm{P}(B)\); “\(A\) given \(B\)”
\(\mathrm{E}(X)\) the expectation (mean) of \(X\) also written \(\mu\)
\(\mathrm{Var}(X)\) the variance of \(X\) \(\mathrm{E}(X^2) - \mathrm{E}(X)^2\); also written \(\sigma^2\)
\(\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y)\) covariance \(\mathrm{E}(XY) - \mathrm{E}(X)\mathrm{E}(Y)\)
\(\mathrm{Corr}(X, Y)\) correlation \(\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y)/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)}\); also written \(\rho\)
\(X \sim \ldots\) \(X\) has the distribution … \(X \sim \mathrm{B}(16, \frac12)\); “\(X\) is distributed as …”
\(\mathrm{B}(n, p)\) binomial distribution number of successes in \(n\) trials
\(\mathrm{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) normal distribution, mean \(\mu\), variance \(\sigma^2\)
\(\mathrm{f}(x)\), \(\mathrm{F}(x)\) probability density function; cumulative distribution function \(\mathrm{F}(x) = \mathrm{P}(X \leq x)\)
\(\mathrm{G}(t)\) probability generating function \(\mathrm{G}(t) = \mathrm{E}(t^X)\)
\(\mathrm{M}_X(\theta)\) moment generating function \(\mathrm{M}_X(\theta) = \mathrm{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\theta X})\)
\(\bar x\) the mean of a sample \(\bar x = \frac1n\sum x_i\)
\(\hat\theta\) an estimate of the unknown quantity \(\theta\) “theta hat”

The Greek alphabet

Greek letters are used throughout mathematics, often with conventional meanings. The table gives the lower-case and capital forms (capitals that look like Roman letters, such as A for alpha, are omitted, since they are not used as separate symbols), the name, and some typical uses.

Letter Name Typical uses
\(\alpha\) alpha angles; roots of an equation
\(\beta\) beta angles; roots of an equation; the beta function
\(\gamma\), \(\Gamma\) gamma Euler’s constant \(\gamma \approx 0.5772\); the gamma function \(\Gamma\); in relativity, \(\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\)
\(\delta\), \(\Delta\) delta a small quantity; \(\Delta\) for a change, or for a triangle or discriminant
\(\varepsilon\), \(\epsilon\) epsilon a small positive quantity; in mechanics, a phase angle
\(\zeta\) zeta the Riemann zeta function, \(\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}\)
\(\eta\) eta efficiency; a small quantity
\(\theta\), \(\Theta\) theta angles; an unknown parameter in statistics
\(\iota\) iota rarely used
\(\kappa\) kappa curvature; constants
\(\lambda\), \(\Lambda\) lambda eigenvalues; a parameter, such as a Poisson rate; wavelength
\(\mu\) mu a mean; the coefficient of friction
\(\nu\) nu frequency
\(\xi\), \(\Xi\) xi a variable or random quantity
\(\pi\), \(\Pi\) pi \(\pi \approx 3.14159\); \(\Pi\) for a product
\(\rho\) rho density; correlation; distance from an origin
\(\sigma\), \(\Sigma\) sigma standard deviation; \(\Sigma\) for a sum
\(\tau\) tau time; torque
\(\upsilon\), \(\Upsilon\) upsilon rarely used
\(\phi\), \(\varphi\), \(\Phi\) phi angles; the golden ratio \(\phi = \frac12(1 + \sqrt5)\); rapidity; \(\Phi\) for the standard normal distribution function
\(\chi\) chi the \(\chi^2\) (chi-squared) distribution
\(\psi\), \(\Psi\) psi a wave function in quantum mechanics
\(\omega\), \(\Omega\) omega angular velocity or frequency; a cube root of unity; \(\Omega\) for a sample space

Mechanics

Symbol Meaning Example or reading
\(g\) acceleration due to gravity about \(9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) at the Earth’s surface
\(G\) the gravitational constant force \(Gm_1m_2/r^2\) between two masses
\(e\) coefficient of restitution (in italics, not to be confused with \(\mathrm{e}\)) speed of separation \(= e \times\) speed of approach
\(\omega\) angular velocity; angular frequency \(\ddot x = -\omega^2x\) for simple harmonic motion
\(I\) moment of inertia rotational energy \(\frac12I\omega^2\) (Section 11.7)
\(c\) the speed of light about \(3 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
\(\hbar\) Planck’s constant divided by \(2\pi\) “h bar” (Section 8.6)
